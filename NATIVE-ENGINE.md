# NIKO Native V5 candidate

The live app now imports niko-native.js and the angle-based NikoGesture. The old engine.js remains in Git and its regression tests, but is excluded from the static build and service worker. NOTICE.txt and LICENSE.md are unchanged: this is not a legal determination of independent authorship, and no existing MIT rights are withdrawn. The landmark conventions and other third-party dependencies remain applicable. The old source was inspected for compatibility; no certified clean-room process.

Cadence uses bilateral knee-flexion angle differences, timestamp-based smoothing, alternating threshold crossings, median inter-event interval and dispersion rejection. Counts are detected alternations, not measured footsteps. The estimator assumes alternating knee motion and can misinterpret other rhythmic movements; no physical reference or clinical validation. Low-rate, occluded or irregular signals can be rejected. Thresholds are experimental. The existing measurement gate and encrypted export remain. Robot commands retain their existing virtual UI semantics; no physical actuation or claim of a fully independent robot engine.

Synthetic comparison is limited to one knee-phase fixture; it does not establish superior real-world accuracy. Prior source code and tests remain accessible in the repository. No change of license or deployment without approval.

## V5.1 — Quality gate
Interpolated crossing timestamps; same-phase half-period estimation; rejects irregular cycles and sampling gaps above 160 ms. Synthetic benchmark: 4 known rates (90/120/150/180), 3 noise amplitudes, deterministic seeds, irregular sampling; acceptance coverage >=75%, MAE <=5 alternations/min; zero outputs on simultaneous flexion and stationary controls. Legacy results are logged for comparison without claiming clinical superiority. This is a regression fixture, not a held-out human dataset. Actual video and reference instrument validation remain mandatory. Low frame-rate devices may receive fewer estimates deliberately.

## V6 adaptive candidate
Robust rolling quantiles estimate the center and threshold of bilateral knee phase differences. The estimator requires sufficient amplitude on each side and negative correlation, rejecting unilateral and simultaneous motion. It keeps bounded 3.2 s history and recomputes statistics every 250 ms. Old V5.1 remains a comparison baseline. Candidate tests add low-amplitude and asymmetric offset synthetic fixtures, plus standard and negative controls. These fixtures are not a clinical dataset, and bilateral image landmarks are not independent sensors. Application still requires bilateral visibility and a six-second quality window. No claim of superiority until comparative CI results are available; no measured CPU or physical camera performance yet.
